# Mill City Web Studio — Brand Direction

## Working name

**Mill City Web Studio**

## Positioning

An independent Minneapolis web studio focused on thoughtful, practical websites for small businesses.

The studio should feel experienced and professional without looking or sounding like a large agency. Clients work directly with the person designing and building the site.

## Core message

**Thoughtful websites for small businesses.**

Supporting ideas:

- small-business websites built with 20+ years of software engineering experience
- direct, approachable communication
- custom design without unnecessary agency overhead
- modern hosting and maintainable technical foundations
- a limited project list so each client gets focused attention

## Minneapolis story

The Mill City name is both a Minneapolis reference and a personal one.

The founder is based in Minneapolis. He and his wife got engaged on the Stone Arch Bridge and were married nearby at St. Anthony Main. They are raising three children in the Minneapolis area and spend much of their family time around youth sports. He also follows the Minnesota Timberwolves and Minnesota Twins.

This story should appear naturally on the About page. It should add warmth and authenticity without becoming the primary sales message.

## Visual direction

Use a modern Minneapolis / industrial-refined aesthetic rather than literal historic-mill branding.

Current palette:

- warm limestone / paper background
- charcoal ink
- muted Mississippi blue
- restrained copper / brick accent

Visual references can include subtle geometry inspired by:

- Stone Arch Bridge arches
- river lines
- mill-grid / architectural patterns
- limestone and brick textures

Avoid:

- generic tech-company gradients
- faux-rustic lumberjack aesthetics
- overly corporate agency language
- sports-team colors or logos as branding
- literal landmark illustrations unless they are original or properly licensed

## Voice

Clear, friendly, capable, and grounded.

Prefer:

- “You work directly with the person building your site.”
- “Professional web work without the agency runaround.”
- “A practical website partner for a small business.”

Avoid:

- “full-service digital transformation agency”
- “cutting-edge solutions”
- inflated claims about team size or capabilities
- jargon when plain language works better
